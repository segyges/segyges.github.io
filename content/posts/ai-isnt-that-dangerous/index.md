AI companies, on the other hand, are reasonably dangerous.

It seems like everyone started worrying about AI ending the world in about the last week or two. It used to be some people, and now it’s everyone. My best guess is that the AI doom stuff made it into the news or YouTube and now everyone’s worried that we’re in a Terminator movie.

So here’s basically what I think I would say to, for example, someone who just bought a bunch of ammunition because they think the power grid might go out.

## Rogue AI Aren’t Real

I’m pretty sure we’re not in a Terminator movie because you can just unplug the AI we have now. It needs a lot of electricity, a lot of hardware, and a lot of maintenance, all from humans, pretty much around the clock all day every day. If we decide we’re unhappy with an AI, it just gets turned off.

If someone invents an AI that can go get its own electricity, its own parts and its own giant air-conditioned warehouse surrounded by tall fencing and armed guards, then you should be a little worried about the AI going rogue. We’re nowhere close to that. It’s fine.

Rogue AI isn’t a real thing that exists. What do exist are AI that someone forgot to turn off or [wasn’t paying attention to](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/aug/26/openai-staff-observed-warning-signs-before-ai-agent-hacking-crusade-caused-global-alarm), even though it costs millions of dollars to keep them on.

There’s a really easy solution to that one: make people watch what they’re doing. Like we do with airlines, pharmacists, banks and power plants. All of those things basically work as long as someone has their eye on them.

Mostly, [people are keeping their eye on their AI now](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-institutes-new-safeguards-after-hugging-face-breach/), even without the law making them. It turns out it only takes one big example for people to not want their company to get in trouble for leaving their AI totally unsupervised. We had [one big incident in July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_rogue_agent_breach_of_Hugging_Face), and no big ones since, and that’s why.

Also, AI pretty much does what people tell it to do, and acts how people tell it to act. This was supposed to be a hard problem, and it’s not, really. Or maybe it’s just not a hard problem anymore, because the work that went into it … worked. You train them to follow instructions and they follow instructions.

When things go wrong, it’s usually because the instructions were bad. The “rogue AI” that have hacked into important things were told to hack into things! They just hacked into the wrong things, which is a reasonable mistake. It didn’t just decide to do that entirely on its own.[^1]

The smarter the AI gets, the better it gets at figuring out what you really want it to do, and not doing the wrong thing instead. It doesn’t decide to be evil because it’s smart now. It turns out that’s not how it works.

Scary stories get more clicks and sell more subscriptions than boring ones. All of this is complicated enough that you can make it sound much scarier than it is without outright *lying*. All of the scary headlines about rogue AI are still, at least, misleading. We know what went wrong here, and we have a pretty good idea how to prevent it from happening in the future. It’s a manageable problem.

## Computers Are Getting Safer

Recent hacking stuff isn’t that big of a deal, and things are basically fine.

Hacking works by finding mistakes in computer code. If you fix the mistake, the program can’t be hacked anymore. If an AI can find the mistake to break into something, it can also find that same mistake and fix it so that no one else can.

There are a lot more people working on fixing security problems than there are working on taking advantage of them. There’s also a lot more money behind it. We are getting closer and closer to making it impossible to hack into anything ever again. We are probably most of the way there.

Only downside is, security reviews are a lot of work and they kind of came in all at once, so everyone working on security is overworked and they seem pretty stressed out. If you’re in the trenches, this is a huge deal, but because so many people are working on it and most of them are good at their jobs, it should only be this big of a deal this one time. Once we’re done fixing all the problems that we can find with AI, security will be much better, and we can keep it that way.

It’s been just a bit over six months since AI became an essential tool for computer security, and nothing very bad has happened. We’re probably almost out of the woods on this one.

Also, everything really essential is already pretty hard to hack into. Do you know how paranoid the people who run power substations are? They wake up in a cold sweat worrying that it isn’t exactly perfect. Essential gear generally isn’t even on the internet, and someone would have to get past a barbed wire fence and an armed guard to even try to do anything to it.

It’s true that the airlines messed up one of their computer updates, but that’s happened before. [In 2024, a bad software update messed up every airline’s computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CrowdStrike-related_IT_outages), and it screwed up airline bookings, too. But that’s a normal human screw-up, and it’s a booking system, not a safety system! Your worst case is that you can’t fly that day, not that the plane falls out of the sky. Plane controls aren’t on the internet, for obvious reasons.

Most other recent outages [appear to be security upgrades](https://apnews.com/live/trump-asia-philippines-politics-news-updates-11-17-2026#000001a1-eba8-d73e-a9a7-efff86230000). How this happens is that a bank (or someone) runs a security audit with brand-new tools, their audit finds a problem, and they decide that fixing the problem is so important that they’re willing to take down their site and app if that’s what it takes to fix it. It’s inconvenient for everyone, but after that single day of inconvenience it’s a good thing. That security problem was already there, they just didn’t have any good way to find it until now.

## The Bad News

AI isn’t that dangerous.

AI companies are reasonably dangerous.

The first and most important customer for AI companies seems to be the government. If you don’t really trust the government, this is a problem. AI is especially good for surveillance. Once upon a time, you had to pay a person to listen to every single call and read every single text message if you wanted to know what was in them. Now, you can have an AI do most of the work cheap.

Your fast solution for that is to use encrypted apps that can’t be cracked, where [Signal](https://signal.org/install/) is your best bet.[^2] Your good solution is to have a government that you can trust. (Good luck). Your medium solution is to have laws, with good enforcement, about when and if they’re allowed to spy on you. They’re supposed to have to get warrants. Less spying would be better, in my opinion.

AI companies also have a ton of money, and money is power, so AI companies are a brand-new trillion-dollar-plus blob of power. I would be less worried about this if everyone involved seemed sane. Unfortunately, a bunch of them [are in a cult](https://www.verysane.ai/p/ai-safety-is-mostly-a-sex-cult-in-berkeley-california), most of them seem to believe that science fiction is a documentary, and some of them seem to be sponsoring a ton of YouTubers to tell people that we’re all going to die.[^3]

Possibly the biggest problem is how many of them seem to be really into the idea of having some kind of arms race and maybe war with China. I think that having a war, cold or otherwise, is pretty clearly unnecessary. We only started talking about Chinese AI as if it’s an arms race and a new cold war because AI companies were saying it. War is big business. You can make a lot of money as a defense contractor. What could be a better business move than creating the problem and then selling the solution for it?

It’s definitely better for us if our technology and our companies are the best, but claiming that your foreign competition is in an arms race with America shouldn’t be taken at face value.

## Bottom Line

When I worry about what’s going on in AI, I’m barely worried about the AI. I’m worried about who’s making it, and what they’re trying to do.

The AI can’t grow on its own, but an AI company can. It can buy as much electricity and as much hardware as it can find for sale. Companies are meant to grow, and growing is their entire purpose. AI corporations, which are in part run by their internal AI, actually could escape control and plot against humanity.

Rogue AI aren’t close to being real, but rogue AI companies are.

On the off chance some future AI could be dangerous on its own without a corporation full of humans, I want whoever is at the wheel to be sane. Someone has to spend a lot of time and probably money making it, and then they have to either botch their security or decide it’s a good idea to turn it loose. I want whoever is in charge to be careful and professional. Ideally, they should have an accountant’s personality. I want to trust them like I trust my bank.

In fact, I would hope that any actually risky research had a reasonable security audit done by reliable professionals who owe them nothing. Existing proposals are primarily to have [other people closely affiliated with AI companies](https://www.verysane.ai/p/is-metr-a-meaningful-check-on-anthropic) audit them, and I don’t think that’s going to cut it. Either the audit should be as serious as an accounting and security audit of a bank, or it isn’t a real audit.

If we are smart about managing technology, it can make us happier, safer, healthier, richer and more free. Because of how fast AI is moving, it’s harder to keep track of it, but it isn’t fundamentally different here. It’s important for us to be practical, to focus on exactly who is likely to cause exactly what problem, and choose to do things that actually solve those problems.
